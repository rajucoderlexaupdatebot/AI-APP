PART 1: TRANSFER OF PROPERTY ACT, 1882 (TPA)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OVERVIEW:
The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 governs the transfer of property between
living persons in India. It defines the rules, conditions, and restrictions
applicable to property transactions.

KEY DEFINITIONS (Section 3):
- "Immovable property" includes land, benefits arising out of land, and things
  attached to the earth or permanently fastened to anything attached to the earth.
- "Instrument" means a non-testamentary document.
- "Attestation" means signed by two or more witnesses, each of whom has seen
  the executant sign or affix his mark to the instrument.
- "Registered" means registered under the law for registration of documents.
- "Actionable claim" means a claim to any debt (other than a debt secured by
  mortgage or charge upon immoveable property).

SECTION 5 — TRANSFER OF PROPERTY DEFINED:
In the following sections, "transfer of property" means an act by which a
living person conveys property, in present or in future, to one or more other
living persons, or to himself and one or more other living persons.

SECTION 6 — WHAT MAY BE TRANSFERRED:
Property of any kind may be transferred, except:
(a) The chance of an heir-apparent succeeding to an estate.
(b) The chance of a relation obtaining a legacy.
(c) A mere right of re-entry for breach of a condition subsequent.
(d) An easement apart from the dominant heritage.
(e) An interest in property restricted in its enjoyment to the owner personally.
(f) A right to future maintenance.
(g) A mere right to sue cannot be transferred.
(h) A public office cannot be transferred.

SECTION 7 — PERSONS COMPETENT TO TRANSFER:
Every person competent to contract, and entitled to transferable property, or
authorised to dispose of transferable property not his own, is competent to
transfer such property in the circumstances and to the extent allowed by law.
- Must be a major (above 18 years as per Indian Majority Act, 1875).
- Must be of sound mind.
- Must not be disqualified by law.

SECTION 8 — OPERATION OF TRANSFER:
Unless a different intention is expressed or implied, a transfer of property
passes forthwith to the transferee all the interest which the transferor is then
capable of passing in the property, and in the legal incidents thereof.

SECTION 54 — SALE DEFINED:
"Sale" is a transfer of ownership in exchange for a price paid or promised or
part-paid and part-promised. A sale of tangible immoveable property of value
of Rs. 100 or more can only be made by a registered instrument.

SECTION 55 — RIGHTS AND LIABILITIES OF BUYER AND SELLER:
Seller must:
(a) Disclose to buyer any material defect in property or title.
(b) Produce title-deeds for examination.
(c) Answer relevant questions about title.
(d) Execute proper conveyance on payment.
(e) Take care of property and title deeds until delivery.

Buyer must:
(a) Disclose material facts increasing value of property known to him.
(b) Pay purchase price at time and place agreed.
(c) Bear loss or destruction of property after sale is complete.

SECTION 58 — MORTGAGE DEFINED:
A mortgage is a transfer of an interest in specific immoveable property for the
purpose of securing the payment of money advanced or to be advanced by way of
loan, an existing or future debt, or the performance of an engagement which may
give rise to a pecuniary liability.
Types of mortgage:
(a) Simple mortgage
(b) Mortgage by conditional sale
(c) Usufructuary mortgage
(d) English mortgage
(e) Mortgage by deposit of title deeds (equitable mortgage)
(f) Anomalous mortgage

SECTION 105 — LEASE DEFINED:
A lease of immoveable property is a transfer of a right to enjoy such property
made for a certain time, express or implied, or in perpetuity, in consideration
of a price paid or promised, or of money, a share of crops, service, or any
other thing of value to be rendered periodically or on specified occasions to
the transferor by the transferee.

SECTION 122 — GIFT DEFINED:
"Gift" is the transfer of certain existing moveable or immoveable property made
voluntarily and without consideration, by one person (donor) to another (donee),
and accepted by or on behalf of the donee. Acceptance must occur during donor's
lifetime. Gift of immoveable property must be by registered instrument.

PART 2: REGISTRATION ACT, 1908
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OVERVIEW:
The Registration Act, 1908 provides for the registration of documents to ensure
authenticity, prevent fraud, and maintain a public record of transactions
involving immoveable property.

SECTION 17 — DOCUMENTS OF WHICH REGISTRATION IS COMPULSORY:
The following documents must be compulsorily registered:
(a) Instruments of gift of immoveable property.
(b) Other non-testamentary instruments which purport or operate to create,
    declare, assign, limit, or extinguish any right, title, or interest of
    value of Rs. 100 or more to or in immoveable property.
(c) Non-testamentary instruments which acknowledge the receipt or payment of
    any consideration on account of creation, declaration, assignment,
    limitation, or extinction of any such right, title, or interest.
(d) Leases of immoveable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding
    one year, or reserving a yearly rent.
(e) Non-testamentary instruments transferring or assigning any decree or order
    of Court, or any award, when such decree or order or award purports or
    operates to create, declare, assign, limit, or extinguish any right, title,
    or interest to or in immoveable property of value of Rs. 100 or more.

SECTION 18 — DOCUMENTS OF WHICH REGISTRATION IS OPTIONAL:
The following documents may be registered:
(a) Instruments (other than wills) relating to immoveable property of value
    less than Rs. 100.
(b) Instruments acknowledging satisfaction of a mortgage.
(c) Leases of immoveable property for a term not exceeding one year.
(d) Wills.
(e) Authority to adopt.
(f) Documents other than those referred to in Section 17.

SECTION 23 — TIME FOR PRESENTING DOCUMENTS:
Subject to provisions of Sections 24, 25 and 26, no document other than a will
shall be accepted for registration unless presented for that purpose within
four months from the date of its execution.

SECTION 25 — PROVISION WHERE DELAY IN PRESENTATION IS CAUSED BY UNAVOIDABLE ACCIDENT:
If owing to urgent necessity or unavoidable accident, any document executed in
India is not presented for registration within the time hereinbefore specified,
the Registrar, in cases where the delay does not exceed four months, may allow
registration on payment of a fine not exceeding ten times the amount of proper
registration fees.

SECTION 49 — EFFECT OF NON-REGISTRATION OF DOCUMENTS REQUIRED TO BE REGISTERED:
No document required by Section 17 to be registered shall:
(a) Affect any immoveable property comprised therein.
(b) Confer any power to adopt.
(c) Be received as evidence of any transaction affecting such property or
    conferring such power.
Unless it has been registered.

SECTION 60 — CERTIFICATE OF REGISTRATION:
The Registering Officer shall endorse thereon a certificate containing the word
"registered" together with the number and page of the book in which the document
has been copied, and shall sign and date the certificate and note in the margin
of the Register Book the departure from office.

REGISTRATION PROCEDURE:
1. Document must be presented at the Sub-Registrar's office having jurisdiction.
2. All executants must personally appear before the registering officer.
3. Identity proof and photographs of parties required.
4. Payment of stamp duty and registration fee.
5. Two witnesses must be present and sign the document.
6. Biometric verification may be required in some states.

PART 3: REAL ESTATE (REGULATION AND DEVELOPMENT) ACT, 2016 (RERA)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OVERVIEW:
RERA was enacted to regulate the real estate sector in India, protect the
interests of consumers, and promote transparency and accountability in
real estate transactions.

KEY DEFINITIONS (Section 2):
- "Allottee" means the person to whom a plot, apartment, or building has been
  allotted, sold or otherwise transferred.
- "Promoter" means a person who constructs or causes to be constructed an
  apartment/building for the purpose of selling.
- "Real estate project" means the development of a building or a plot for sale.
- "Carpet area" means the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding
  area covered by external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony
  or verandah area, and exclusive open terrace area.

SECTION 3 — PRIOR REGISTRATION OF REAL ESTATE PROJECT WITH AUTHORITY:
No promoter shall advertise, market, book, sell, or offer for sale or invite
any person to purchase any plot, apartment, or building in any real estate
project without registering the real estate project with the Real Estate
Regulatory Authority.

SECTION 4 — APPLICATION FOR REGISTRATION OF REAL ESTATE PROJECTS:
Every promoter shall make an application for registration accompanied by:
(a) Authenticated copy of approvals and commencement certificate.
(b) Sanctioned plan and layout plan.
(c) Plan of development works.
(d) Proposed facilities including firefighting facilities, drinking water
    facilities, emergency evacuation services, use of renewable energy.
(e) Firm and total amount to be paid by allottee.
(f) Number, type, and carpet area of apartments.
(g) Names and addresses of real estate agents.
(h) Declarations by promoter.

SECTION 11 — FUNCTIONS AND DUTIES OF PROMOTER:
Promoter shall:
(a) Make the application for registration of the real estate project.
(b) Create a webpage on RERA website containing project details.
(c) Maintain separate accounts for 70% of amounts realised in an escrow account.
(d) Not accept more than 10% of the cost of apartment as advance without
    first entering into a written agreement for sale.
(e) Execute registered agreement for sale with allottee.
(f) Obtain completion certificate and provide it to allottee.
(g) Obtain occupation certificate and provide it to allottee.
(h) Maintain essential services until formation of association of allottees.

SECTION 14 — ADHERENCE TO SANCTIONED PLANS:
The promoter shall develop and complete the real estate project in accordance
with the sanctioned plans, layout plans, and specifications as approved by
competent authorities. No addition or alteration without consent of 2/3rd
of allottees after occupancy.

SECTION 18 — RETURN OF AMOUNT AND COMPENSATION:
If the promoter fails to complete or is unable to give possession of an apartment:
(a) In accordance with terms of agreement for sale, or
(b) Due to discontinuance of his business or insolvency,
The promoter shall be liable on demand to the allottees, in case the allottee
wishes to withdraw from the project, without prejudice to any other remedy,
to return the amount received by him plus interest at such rate as may be
prescribed, and compensation in the manner provided.

SECTION 19 — RIGHTS AND DUTIES OF ALLOTTEES:
Allottees shall:
(a) Have the right to obtain information relating to sanctioned plans, layout
    plans, specifications, and stage-wise time schedule of completion.
(b) Have the right to know stage-wise time schedule of completion of project.
(c) Have the right to claim possession of apartment/plot.
(d) Have the right to claim refund with interest and compensation.
(e) Pay instalments within time and manner specified in agreement.
(f) Pay interest for delay in payment.
(g) Participate in formation of the association.

RERA REGISTRATION CHECK:
Always verify:
- Whether the project is registered under state RERA authority.
- RERA registration number (format varies by state).
- Project completion date and current status.
- Whether promoter has uploaded all required documents on RERA portal.
- Escrow account compliance status.

PART 4: STAMP DUTY — KEY PRINCIPLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OVERVIEW:
Stamp duty is a state subject. Rates vary by state. Common rates in major states:

KARNATAKA:
- Sale deed: 5% of property value + 1% registration fee + 0.5% BBMP / local
  authority surcharge.
- Gift deed (to family): 1% of property value.
- Gift deed (to non-family): 5% of property value.
- Power of attorney: Rs. 500 (limited) or 5% (general, to sell property).

MAHARASHTRA:
- Sale deed: 5% of property value (market value or agreement value, whichever
  is higher) + 1% local body surcharge.
- Women buyers: 1% concession in certain cases.

DELHI:
- Men: 6% of property value.
- Women: 4% of property value.
- Registration fee: 1% (max Rs. 1,00,000).

IMPORTANT STAMP DUTY RULES:
- Stamp duty is payable on the higher of: agreement value or government circle
  rate (also called guideline value / ready reckoner rate).
- Under-stamping is a legal defect. A document not properly stamped is
  inadmissible in court and is treated as invalid.
- Deficient stamp duty attracts penalty up to 10 times the deficient amount.
- Stamp duty must be paid before or at time of execution of document.

PART 5: COMMON LEGAL RISKS IN PROPERTY TRANSACTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TITLE DEFECTS:
1. Unclear chain of title — gaps in ownership history.
2. Forged or fabricated documents.
3. Impersonation — seller pretending to be the true owner.
4. Property sold by a person without legal authority (no valid Power of Attorney).
5. Property belonging to a minor — requires court permission for sale.
6. Property belonging to a person of unsound mind — requires court approval.
7. Property sold without consent of co-owners (in jointly held property).
8. Court-attached property sold without court permission.
9. Property under government acquisition notification.
10. Benami transactions — property held in another's name.

ENCUMBRANCE RISKS:
1. Mortgage or charge not disclosed by seller.
2. Pending loan against the property (banks have first charge).
3. Unpaid property tax or water tax.
4. Unpaid society maintenance (in apartments).
5. Legal dispute pending in court (lis pendens).
6. Caveat lodged by a third party.

DOCUMENTATION RISKS:
1. Sale deed not properly stamped.
2. Sale deed not registered.
3. Missing link documents (breaks in chain of title).
4. Documents in regional language not properly translated.
5. Power of Attorney: expired, revoked, or not properly executed.
6. Khata/mutation not updated in the seller's name.
7. Plan approval not obtained or lapsed.
8. Occupancy certificate not obtained.

RERA-SPECIFIC RISKS:
1. Project not registered under RERA.
2. Project registration expired.
3. Builder has record of delayed or incomplete projects.
4. Agreement for sale not in RERA-prescribed format.
5. Carpet area not clearly defined.

PART 6: ENCUMBRANCE CERTIFICATE (EC) — GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IS AN EC:
An Encumbrance Certificate is an official document issued by the Sub-Registrar's
office confirming all registered transactions on a property during a given period.
It shows all charges, mortgages, or liabilities registered against the property.

HOW TO OBTAIN:
- Apply at Sub-Registrar's office or online (state-specific portals).
- Specify property survey number and required period (minimum 13 years recommended).
- Fee varies by state (typically Rs. 200–500 plus Rs. 25 per year of search).

WHAT EC SHOWS:
- All registered sales of the property.
- All registered mortgages.
- All registered lease deeds.
- All registered gift deeds.
- Court attachments (if separately registered).

WHAT EC DOES NOT SHOW:
- Unregistered transactions.
- Court orders (unless specifically registered).
- Government acquisition orders (check separately with revenue dept.).
- Tax dues.
- Society dues.
- Family disputes / oral agreements.

IMPORTANT: Always obtain EC for at least 30 years. 13 years is minimum.

PART 7: POWER OF ATTORNEY (POA) — KEY LEGAL POINTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TYPES OF POA:
1. General Power of Attorney (GPA) — broad powers including power to sell.
2. Special Power of Attorney (SPA) — restricted to specific acts.
3. Irrevocable POA — cannot be revoked (only valid in specific circumstances).

LEGAL REQUIREMENTS FOR POA IN PROPERTY TRANSACTIONS:
- Must be executed by the principal (owner) in the presence of a Notary.
- Should be registered if used for selling immoveable property (Supreme Court:
  Suraj Lamp & Industries v. State of Haryana, 2011).
- Must be stamped as per applicable Stamp Act.
- Agent must be specifically authorised to execute sale deeds.

RISKS WITH POA-BASED PROPERTY TRANSACTIONS:
- POA may have been revoked before use.
- Principal may have died, automatically revoking the POA.
- POA may be forged.
- POA may not authorise the specific act being performed.
- Supreme Court has held that GPA sales do not confer marketable title.
  (Suraj Lamp & Industries Pvt. Ltd. v State of Haryana & Another [2011] 14 SCC 656)

VERIFICATION STEPS FOR POA:
- Confirm POA is registered (for property transactions).
- Confirm POA has not been revoked.
- Confirm principal is alive and competent.
- Check specific powers granted.
- Verify agent's identity matches.

PART 8: DUE DILIGENCE CHECKLIST
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PRE-PURCHASE DUE DILIGENCE STEPS:
1. Verify title by examining 30 years of title documents.
2. Obtain and verify Encumbrance Certificate for 30 years.
3. Obtain mutation/Khata certificate confirming property in seller's name.
4. Verify seller's identity (Aadhaar, PAN, passport).
5. Confirm property tax receipts are up to date.
6. Check for lis pendens (pending litigation) at court records.
7. Verify building plan approval and its validity.
8. Obtain and verify Occupancy Certificate (for completed buildings).
9. For RERA projects: verify registration and check project status online.
10. For apartments: verify No Dues Certificate from society.
11. Check for any government acquisition notifications.
12. Verify zonal classification (residential / commercial / agricultural).
13. For agricultural land: check if conversion to residential use has been obtained.
14. Verify all co-owners have signed / consented.
15. If POA: follow POA verification steps above.
16. Check that stamp duty was correctly paid on previous transactions.
17. Verify registration of all previous sale deeds in the title chain.

DOCUMENTS TO COLLECT AND VERIFY:
- Original sale deed / mother deed.
- All link documents (chain of title).
- Encumbrance Certificate.
- Khata / mutation records.
- Property tax paid receipts.
- Building plan approval.
- Commencement certificate.
- Occupancy / completion certificate.
- RERA registration certificate (if applicable).
- Society NOC / No Dues Certificate (if applicable).
- Latest utility bills (electricity, water).
